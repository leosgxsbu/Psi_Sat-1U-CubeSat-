Overview

LEO-GRM (Low-Earth Orbit Gamma-Ray Mapping) is a Stony Brook University project to design a 1U CubeSat observatory for soft gamma rays and hard X-rays. The payload couples a 50 × 50 × 3 mm CsI(Tl) scintillator to a Hamamatsu S13360-6050PE SiPM, with pulse timing on an STM32F411RET6 flight computer. Open telemetry is intended for public release through SatNOGS.

Authors (July 2026 proposal): Jackson Boyle, Christian Tumanda, Muhammad Maalik.

Detection performance (proposal)

Energy band30 keV – 300 keV
Crystal50 × 50 × 3 mm CsI(Tl)
Absorption (100 keV, μ = 6.21 cm⁻¹)α ≈ 84.5%
Crystal yield~54 photons/keV (~550 nm)
Energy efficiency η≈ 12.15%
Fluence threshold10⁻⁶ erg/cm²
Software triggerMean + 5σ rolling background
Projected rate~10–20 soft GRBs/year

Background rejection uses a rolling 5σ threshold: Trigger = Mean + 5σ. Burst duration is inferred from pulse timestamps on EXTI10 (short events ~1–5 s; longer events up to ~40–60 s in proposal examples).

Current build status: we are designing the board stack. The STM32 flight computer board (Stage 3) schematic is nearly complete. Next, we'll do a low‑cost bench test of the CsI(Tl) crystal + SiPM detector and create a development board for flight software, before any flight hardware.

CSLI timeline

NASA's CubeSat Launch Initiative page (updated Oct 14, 2026) indicates the next CSLI solicitation is anticipated in 2028. PsiSAT is now targeting the 2028 call. IARU coordination and FCC planning will proceed alongside design work.
